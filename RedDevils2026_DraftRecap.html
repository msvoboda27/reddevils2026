Setting the Scene

The 40th. Nobody Can Believe It.

Seven guys from Lowell, Indiana. Met in high school. And here they are, four decades into a fantasy baseball league that has outlasted jobs, marriages, bad picks, and approximately one lifetime's worth of Portillo's beef sandwiches. The 40th RedDevils Draft kicked off at 11 AM sharp on a March Saturday and wrapped up ten hours later — full, loud, and already debating next year.

Look at that photo. Back row: Rich Mager on the left — the defending 2025 champion, wearing the quiet confidence of a man who knows the entire room is plotting against him and genuinely does not care. Next to him, Furman, who has assembled what might be the finest roster of his 20-year RedDevils career and still has not won a single title, which is either a character-building exercise or a cosmic joke at this point. Then Bubs (Dale), yellow hat, Aaron Judge already locked, completely at peace with the universe. And on the far back right: Mic "Quickster" Svoboda — the man who built the entire draft app from scratch, told the room with full confidence that Claude will not fail, and then at pick 3, with the whole league watching, selected Tarik Skubal over Juan Soto. Claude did not fail. The AI is fine. Quickster's draft decision-making is a separate conversation.

Front row: Cone on the left in the IU hoodie — back-to-back champion, namer of teams exclusively after classic rock albums (WAR, KICK, Pyromania, Murmur — a pattern so brazenly consistent it has become genuinely impressive). Center front, TK / Tim Kaiser in the Cubs hat — four titles, a nickname so stubbornly unoriginal that it has somehow achieved dynasty status, the Cool Breez cannot be argued with. And on the far right, Vic — arms folded, already done deliberating, The Shockers era officially retired, FAFO fully activated, and Juan Soto sitting on his roster because Quickster handed him the pick.

"Seven guys who met in high school in Lowell still find a way to get together once a year. That's not a fantasy league. That's a friendship league with baseball attached."

— Quickster (Mic Svoboda), on what this day is actually about
The Draft Itself

Pick by Pick: The Moments That Defined the Day

Ten hours. 147 picks. One draft room. The elapsed times tell their own story — some picks took 10 seconds (confident, or desperate?), one took nearly 7 minutes (Bubs, Round 14, Westburg — a deliberation for the ages). Here are the moments that mattered.

Pick1.01
Bubs Takes Judge. Nobody Is Shocked.
In a move that surprised absolutely no one who has watched Bubs draft for 40 years, the man with an elite outfielder in six of seven recent first rounds did it again. Aaron Judge, #1 overall. Bubs has been drafting with the elegance of a man reading from a script he wrote himself. The script says: take the best outfielder. Every. Single. Year.
Pick1.02
Furman Gets Ohtani (Batter). Still No Ring.
Tom Furman — LOVE, HURTS — has picked second in five of the last seven years and has precisely zero championships to show for it. He grabbed Shohei Ohtani (batting) in 10 seconds flat, which is an elite pick. Furman is very good at drafting and historically catastrophic at winning. This is his cross to bear. Twenty years and counting.
⏱ 10 seconds
Pick1.03
Quickster Takes Skubal. Passes on Soto. Has No Regrets (Yet).
Mic had the 3rd pick and chose Tarik Skubal over Juan Soto. His reasoning: keeper pitchers Fried + Kirby already gave him elite SP depth, so adding the #1 pitcher made more sense than a hitter he'd have to build around differently. The pick elapsed in 18 seconds — either brilliant conviction or dangerous overconfidence. Juan Soto was available for exactly one more pick.
⏱ 18 seconds
Pick1.04
Cone Grabs Bobby Witt Jr. The Defending Streak Gets Dangerous.
The back-to-back champion — album-naming enthusiast, IU hoodie philosopher — grabbed Bobby Witt Jr., arguably the best all-around player in baseball. Three minutes and change. For Cone, that might mean he was deliberating between two studs. Or eating something. Probably both.
⏱ 3 min 21 sec
Pick1.05
Vic Gets Juan Soto. In 15 Seconds. You're Welcome, Vic.
Vic — FAFO — quietly picked up the player Mic passed on. Juan Soto, #4 overall. In 15 seconds. Vic has two championships and the quiet menace of a man who doesn't need to announce anything. He drafted the best hitter available, went back to his seat, and probably hasn't thought about it since.
⏱ 15 seconds
Pick2.05
Quickster Lands Kyle Tucker. The Rotation + Lineup Takes Shape.
With pick 12, Mic grabbed Kyle Tucker — #4 OF, #8 overall. Add the Fried/Kirby keepers, Skubal, and now Tucker, and the team is already taking a very dangerous shape. Yordan Alvarez and Bryce Harper would follow. The Bears hoodie was hiding a seriously loaded roster.
⏱ 60 seconds
Pick6.07
Bubs Takes Wheeler in Round 6. Eight Minutes of Questions.
Bubs took Zack Wheeler at pick 6.07 — ranked #45 SP and #156 overall. That's a significant reach for a drafter known for following the rankings. The pick consumed 8 minutes and 15 seconds of clock, which is an eternity in a live draft and tells you everything about the debate happening inside that Cubs hat. The room waited. The beef waited. The pick landed.
⏱ 8 min 15 sec — longest pick of Round 6
Pick11.05
Vic Gets Ohtani the Pitcher. The Universe Splits Shohei in Two.
Furman has Ohtani the batter. Vic now has Ohtani the pitcher. In this league, Shohei Ohtani exists as two separate beings across two separate rosters — a philosophical experiment that has probably been debated for the rest of the evening. The baseball gods are entertained.
⏱ 2 min 25 sec
Pick14.07
Jordan Westburg to Bubs. Six Minutes, Thirty-Six Seconds of Pure Drama.
The single longest deliberation of the entire 10-hour draft. 6 minutes and 36 seconds. The room held its breath. Portillo's wrappers rustled. Someone refilled their drink. TK probably hummed a tune. And then — Westburg. Bubs had made his choice. The draft moved on. History recorded it.
⏱ 6 min 36 sec — LONGEST SINGLE PICK OF THE DRAFT 🏆

The Real MVP of the Day

The Portillo's Report

After 10 hours of drafting — 147 picks, eight bad takes someone will regret by June, one 6-minute-36-second Westburg deliberation, and the philosophical splitting of Shohei Ohtani across two rosters — these seven men did what men who have known each other since high school do: they ate an absolutely unconscionable amount of food from Portillo's.

This year, Portillo's was the only food. No catering, no sides, no supplemental menu. Just Portillo's, in all its glory. And honestly? Correct decision.

🥩
Italian Beef Sandwiches
The cornerstone. The reason. Consumed with the focused intensity of men who had earned them through 10 hours of fantasy labor.
🍝
Huge Meatballs
No buns. This is important. The meatballs stood alone, as meatballs should, commanding the respect they deserve.
🌿
TK's Garden Dog
In a cathedral of beef, TK ordered a garden dog. Nobody said a word. That's 40 years of friendship right there.

The meatballs with no buns deserve special recognition. This is not an oversight. This is a philosophy. Six men ate beef sandwiches. TK had his garden dog. And the meatballs sat there, bunless, proud, asking nothing of anyone. Perfect.

Post-Draft Activities

Trivia, Name That Tune & the Settling of Old Scores

The draft ended. The Portillo's was consumed. And yet no one left. Because why would you? Once a year, these seven men from Lowell get to be 17 again — arguing, laughing, competing over things that mean nothing and everything simultaneously.

So they played Trivia. Seven men who have collectively accumulated several lifetimes of useless knowledge deployed it with championship-level seriousness. Then came Name That Tune — seven men of a certain era, familiar with the same songs, trying to out-remember each other. Cone probably named albums the whole time. TK probably knew every intro. Mic probably protested a ruling. Rich, defending champion of everything, probably won that too.
